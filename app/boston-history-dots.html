How these groups and map checks work

Voting similarity uses Ward hierarchical clustering, the same grouping method as the county and NYC views. Each precinct contributes its Walsh share in 2013, Walsh share in 2017 and Wu share in 2021. Shares exclude other candidates, write-ins and blank ballots. Selecting the other main candidate instead would give the same grouping: the method compares patterns, not a political left-to-right score.

The default groups only precincts whose same-code shape covers at least 99% of both polygons when comparing the City’s 2017 map with each of two historical sources: MGGG’s processed 2012–2016 precinct map and Census 2020 voting districts. Map agreement is a conservative screen, not proof of identical election-day boundaries. Mapping corrections, coastlines and differences between source purposes can fail this check. The screened subset is small and is not representative of all Boston precincts.

“All matching codes” is an exploratory comparison of reported results under the same code. It does not establish that the same territory or voters are being compared. Precincts with no main-candidate votes in any year cannot be grouped. No votes are estimated, split or reassigned.

Group colours are distinct identifiers, not Republican/Democratic labels. Profiles show average precinct support in each year; these are not citywide vote totals. Close split means an average between 45% and 55%. Groups can change when the included elections or precincts change, and are not permanent political blocs.
