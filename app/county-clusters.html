How this works

We compare how each county voted in five presidential elections. Counties with similar results go in the same group. Start with four groups; choose fewer for a broad picture, or more for finer differences. This describes past results, not a prediction.

Group labels use average Democratic support: below 30% is “Very strongly Republican”, 30% to below 40% is “Strongly Republican”, 40% to below 47% is “Leans Republican”, 47% through 53% is “Closely split”, above 53% through 60% is “Leans Democratic”, above 60% through 70% is “Strongly Democratic”, and above 70% is “Very strongly Democratic”. These are descriptions of the group average, not every county in it. Percentages here count votes for the two major parties only.

Statistical details

Ward clustering groups counties with similar levels of Democratic two-party support across the five presidential elections. It uses Euclidean distance between five-value histories. Counties and elections receive equal weight; the inputs are not standardized within each county. This mainly captures partisan leaning, with changes over time contributing to distance.

Four groups is the starting view, not a claim that four is optimal. Compare 2, 3, 4, 5 or 7 groups. Separation is the mean silhouette score (−1 to 1); higher means counties are better separated from other groups. Stability is the adjusted Rand agreement after leaving out each election and refitting the model; 1 means identical groupings. These checks describe this dataset, not future electoral outcomes.
